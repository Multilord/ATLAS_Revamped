## Slide 1 — Solution ATLAS

**Time: 0:00–0:35 | 35 seconds**

**Purpose:** Introduce the team and establish the central idea.

[Face the audience. Briefly gesture towards the project name.]

“Good morning, everyone. We are Team NorthWind, and today we are presenting Solution ATLAS, our AI-powered solution intelligence engine.

Our guiding idea is simple: **find what worked, check what changed, and reuse what is still valid**.

For a support consultant, finding a previous answer is only the beginning. The next question is whether that answer can be trusted in the customer's current environment.

That is the problem we set out to address.”

[Pause briefly, then advance.]

## Slide 2 — The Hidden Cost of Finding the Right Fix

**Time: 0:35–1:40 | 65 seconds**

**Purpose:** Connect fragmented knowledge to the cost of support work.

[Point first to Worldwide Context, then follow the arrows down to Business Impact.]

“The cost of an IT incident extends beyond the time spent repairing it. Employees lose productive time, while consultants spend effort finding, comparing and checking earlier investigations.

The industry figures shown here put that challenge into perspective: three hours and eighteen minutes of productive time lost per incident, and sixty-eight percent citing information silos as a knowledge-management barrier. These provide wider context; they are not measurements of NTT DATA's internal performance.

For the client use case, we focus on three connected challenges: scattered knowledge, repeated investigations and uncertainty about historical fixes.

When a useful resolution remains buried in an old ticket, another consultant may repeat the same investigation. When an earlier workaround is mistaken for a permanent fix, further validation is needed.

The business impact falls on time, cost and consultant capacity.”

[Allow a short pause on Time, Money and Manpower, then advance.]

## Slide 3 — Why Existing Search & AI Are Not Enough?

**Time: 1:40–2:45 | 65 seconds**

**Purpose:** Explain the difference between retrieving similar information and assessing whether it applies.

[Gesture to Traditional Search, then General AI Search.]

“Traditional search helps consultants locate documents, but keyword matching can miss a relevant investigation described in different language. Consultants must still open the results and compare the context themselves.

General AI search can improve this by retrieving related material and summarising it. However, a relevant summary does not automatically establish that a historical procedure fits the current system.

For example, two SAP tickets may describe similar symptoms but involve different releases, configurations or customer customisations. A procedure that worked in one environment may need further checks in another.

We also need to know whether the earlier record describes a verified fix, a temporary workaround or an investigation that never established a cause.

That is the distinction highlighted here: **similarity is not the same as applicability**.

The consultant needs evidence of fit, alongside the retrieved answer.”

[Pause on the bottom statement, then advance.]

## Slide 4 — What Are We Missing? / Our Opportunity

**Time: 2:45–3:30 | 45 seconds**

**Purpose:** Define the opportunity and transition into the solution on slide 5.

[Point to “solution applicability and trust,” then to “Our Opportunity.”]

“This brings us to the opportunity: assess both applicability and trustworthiness.

Applicability asks whether the historical guidance fits the current application, release, process and customisations.

Trust asks what evidence supports it, whether it is current, and whether conflicting or unsuccessful outcomes have been recorded.

Solution ATLAS brings these considerations into the investigation workflow. It helps consultants review relevant evidence, see potential mismatches and decide what to validate next.

The consultant remains responsible for verification and approval.

Let us now walk through how that works in our solution.”

[Advance to slide 5. If handing over, replace the final sentence with: “I will now hand over to my teammate to walk through how the solution works.”]
